Why this course

Organizations aren't short of specialists.
They're short of people who see the whole picture.

Every decision in a business ripples across it. A new marketing campaign changes what operations must produce, what finance must fund, and who human resources must hire. Meanwhile, forces outside the business, from the economy and competition to technology and regulation, keep shifting the ground beneath it. Whether you join a multinational, a fast-growing startup, or one of the many small and medium enterprises that drive emerging economies, employers value people who understand how the parts fit together and how business creates value for society, not just profit.

That's the capability BUS2125 builds. Not just knowledge of what each function does, but the judgment to see how they connect, and to act ethically when they pull in different directions.

Where this takes you

Six roles where connectors are in demand

These capabilities are the foundation for almost any business career, because every role eventually depends on understanding how the rest of the organization works.

Business Analyst
You study how a business operates and recommend how it can work better. BUS2125 gives you the frameworks to map functions, spot internal and external forces, and see where one department's problem starts in another.
Entrepreneur or Small Business Owner
In a young business, you and a small team perform every function yourselves. This course helps you choose the right business structure and plan which functions to build as the business grows.
Project Coordinator
You keep cross-functional work on track. The RACI matrices and project charters you build in BUS2125 are the same tools coordinators use to make roles and responsibilities clear and prevent duplicated effort.
Operations Coordinator
You turn materials, people, and processes into goods and services as efficiently as possible. Understanding how operations connects to sales, finance, and sourcing helps you improve productivity across the whole chain, not just your part of it.
Management Trainee
Many graduate programs rotate you through several departments before you lead one. BUS2125 prepares you to contribute in any function from day one and understand how your work supports the business's objectives.
Sustainability or CSR Coordinator
You help a business meet its responsibilities to people and the planet. This course's focus on ethics, social responsibility, and sustainability builds the judgment to balance profit with positive impact.
Portfolio

Four artifacts that prove you can connect the pieces

The work you complete in BUS2125 builds, step by step, a complete picture of one business you choose. Together, these artifacts show a hiring manager that you can analyze a real organization, explain how it is structured and how its functions work together, and plan for the changes ahead. They also prepare you for your Final Project.

1
Business Profile
An exploration of a business you choose, its functional areas, and its role in society, built using the research resources professionals rely on. It shows you can quickly make sense of an organization you are joining or advising.
2
Business Structure Recommendation
A justified choice of business structure, weighing advantages and disadvantages against the business's strategy and objectives. The kind of reasoning founders, advisors, and managers use when a business is formed or reorganized.
3
Functional Areas Digital Representation
A visual map of the business's functional areas and how they interconnect, now and in a future-proof workplace. It demonstrates that you understand an organization as a system, a perspective employers look for in every role.
4
Emerging Technology and RACI Matrix
A plan for introducing an emerging technology into your business, with a RACI matrix that defines who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed. A practical tool used in project and change management, and a strong talking point in interviews.
